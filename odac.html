ODAC scheduling and NPI

ODAC scheduling and NPI

What fewer or more league games, changed across the whole ODAC, would do to each ODAC team's NPI. A league game ends in a win for one ODAC team and a loss for the other, or in soccer a tie for both. Played against a non-conference opponent instead, both teams can win. The Change results section recalculates every ODAC team's NPI for the results you choose.

League games and opponent strength

The chart shows the average strength of a team's opponents. The team's own NPI is in the tables below. The average mixes the team's league opponents with the non-conference opponents it schedules.

The shaded area is the range of that average. At the left edge, with no league games, the range runs from the weakest non-conference schedule any ODAC team played this season to the strongest. At the right edge, every game is a league game.

For winning percentage, the range ends at .500. For NPI, league schedules still differ. A team never plays itself, and a schedule short of a full round robin skips some ODAC teams. So the low and high ends are the weakest and strongest league schedules.

Choosing a non-conference opponent

The value of one more game depends on how far the opponent's NPI is from the team's, and on which results would count. Each result is weighted by its chance. The chances are fit on this season's Division III results in the sport, from the NPI gap and the site. Those same results set the NPIs, so the chances are somewhat too confident.

    Business rules
    League game
    A regular-season game between two ODAC teams, whether or not the ODAC counts it in its standings. ODAC tournament games count in every NPI, but no scenario removes or replaces them.
    Fewer league games
    A league game can be removed only when two teams meet twice in the regular season. The later meeting comes off both schedules, and each team plays the replacement opponent in its place. A team that plays each league opponent at most once has nothing removed.
    Random draws
    The more league games columns average 200 random draws. One draw is noisy. The non-conference games a team drops can be wins or losses. That choice moves its NPI as much as the added league games do.
    More league games

    Every ODAC team adds the chosen number of league games, from 1 to 5. For each league game it adds, it drops one non-conference game, so its number of games stays the same. A team with fewer non-conference games against Division III teams adds only as many as it has. Games against teams outside Division III do not count in NPI, so no scenario drops them.

    When the league total comes out odd, one randomly drawn team adds one game fewer. Opponents, the home team, and the games given up are drawn at random. A team cannot draw itself.

    Results
    The team with the higher NPI wins each added league game. A team beats the replacement opponent when its NPI is higher than the opponent's.
    Replacement opponent
    A team with the NPI of whoever holds the chosen national rank in the D3 Datacast ratings named in the league summary. The default is rank 100.

    Method
    Game NPI
    Team NPI
    Team NPI is the average of the team's game NPIs, with the location weights above. Once the team has its minimum number of wins, a win below its NPI does not count. The same goes for a loss at or above its NPI, except the team's worst loss, which always counts. A tie counts as half a win and half a loss. The win part follows the rule for wins. The loss part follows the rule for losses.
    What changes
    Every ODAC team's NPI, recalculated together. Teams outside the ODAC keep their current NPI, so these numbers are estimates.
    Rank
    Where the team's new NPI falls among every Division III team, with the other ODAC teams at their new NPIs.
    Sources
    NPI and ranks from D3 Datacast. Scores of ODAC teams' games from the NCAA.com scoreboard.
